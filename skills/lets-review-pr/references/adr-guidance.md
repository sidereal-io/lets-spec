# Published ADR guidance

Source-checked during implementation on 2026-11-06. All three pages were accessible. These are attributed summaries for offline roles; using this file does not mean the role directly consulted the sources. Report any source you tried but could not access. Preserve the target project's established ADR structure.

- [Michael Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions (2011)](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions): records preserve the motivation behind architecturally significant choices affecting structure, non-functional characteristics, dependencies, interfaces or construction techniques. One short record captures a decision and its forces. Consequences include positive, negative and neutral effects; earlier consequences can become later context. Keep superseded decisions discoverable rather than blindly accepting or reversing them.
- [AWS, Appendix: Example ADR](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/architectural-decision-records/appendix.html): the example ties a branching decision to packaged-deployment context, explains why extra branches add unnecessary complexity, and names both versioning benefits and workflow overhead. Its compliance section translates the decision into concrete branch protection and merge-request practices. Use this as an example of rationale and operational consequences, not a requirement to adopt its branching strategy or headings.
- [GDS, Documenting architecture decisions](https://gds-way.digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk/standards/architecture-decisions.html): preserve context and reasoning for future teams, obtain input from affected disciplines/teams, make accepted decisions discoverable, and follow through on implementation. Implementation can reveal new consequences. Clarifications may amend a record; a changed decision already implemented calls for a new record and a supersession link. An ADR is not a complete system description.
